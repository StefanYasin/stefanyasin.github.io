What plant IT taught me about small business

Downtime is measured in dollars per minute in both places. The difference is who feels it.

I spent years running IT where a stopped line was a phone call you never wanted: plant floors, packaging lines, building systems that fed entire facilities. Then I started working with small business, and everyone assumed the stakes were lower.

They are not. The numbers are smaller and the impact is closer.

When a press stops, the company loses production by the minute. When a small office loses its server, the owner loses the weekend, the payroll run, and a piece of their relationship with every client who could not reach them. Both are downtime. One just fits on a spreadsheet.

The lesson that transfers: boring is the goal. In a plant, nobody wants excitement from the SCADA system. Small business deserves the same standard: backups that have been tested, cabling that is labelled, documentation that survives the person who built it, and monitoring that pages you before the client notices.

The second lesson: the person who understands the whole stack wins. In industrial environments, the best troubleshooters knew the mechanical, the electrical, and the controls layer. Small business has the same shape: the machine, the network, the cloud subscription, and the AI tooling that now sits on top. A provider who only knows one layer is a pass-through. The value is in owning the whole chain.

That is why Cloud and Cable exists: plant-floor standards, applied to the businesses that never thought they could afford them.

Written by Stefan Yasin, Hamilton, Ontario.
